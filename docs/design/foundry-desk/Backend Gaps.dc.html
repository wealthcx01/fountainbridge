Foundry Studio: the back-end gaps

The full-scale wireframe (Foundry Studio Full Wireframe) is designed to be built on the fountainbridge front end as it stands at main. This paper lists what the back end must add, gap by gap, with what already exists and a suggested ticket for each. 10 gaps; 4 need only the studio, 3 need the venture box, 3 wait on a decision.

1 — Already in the build

The wireframe leans on machinery that exists and works today: tickets as git files with a parser and status inference (lib/tickets.ts); the attention queue and ticket joining (lib/attention.ts); signed ActiveGraph approvals, attestation and per-approval histories (lib/approvals.ts, lib/activegraph-log.ts, lib/provenance.ts); budgets read from the studio repo (lib/budgets.ts); run reports, engine heartbeats and the held-plan release (lib/runreports.ts, app/actions/release-plan.ts); the composed brief (lib/brief.ts); knowledge and routines (lib/knowledge.ts, lib/routines.ts); the streaming composer with document reads (lib/composer.ts); Google and password sign-in; the FB-009 mobile pass; and the budgeted board polling of WhileWorking.tsx. None of these are gaps. The desk is a re-arrangement of them.

2 — The gaps

G1  The per-ticket trail

The design shows: every ticket followable to where it changed things; a single ordered trail of filed, picked up, commits, checks, preview, approval, deploy or send. Exists: the events, scattered; historyFor joins them per approval only. Build: one endpoint joining ActiveGraph events, run reports and the repo's commits/checks per ticket id, with resolvable links to the diff on the venture VM and the running preview. Suggest: FB-130. Studio-side only.

G2  The Sell trace

The design shows: "follow it to your outbox"; per-send reports (delivered, opened, replied) on the desk. Exists: the send gate (signed approvals); the GTM architecture is ratified in docs/research-gtm.md §7 with Phase 4b tickets drafted. Build: the email service integration, per-campaign report storage, and a deep link into the venture Workspace outbox. Suggest: the drafted FB-02x set, plus FB-131 for the report surface.

G3  The Scale connector

The design shows: Scale honestly not connected; ARCA-16 blocked at the gate with "ad account not connected, platform tbd". Exists: the gate behaviour. Build: the platform decision first (Meta or equivalent), then the connector, spend reporting into budgets, and the account link-through. Suggest: a decision memo before any ticket; FB-132 once decided. Waits on a decision.

G4  Per-ticket composer threads

The design shows: "Discuss in the composer" on every ticket; the thread pinned beside it; agreed changes filed as revisions the trail cites. Exists: the ?about= seed (FB-105) and the one gated write path; transcripts live only in the browser (FB-065, by design). Build: server-side threads keyed by ticket in the venture repo's context/ (D8), and a file-revision action that records the conversation as source. Suggest: FB-133. Studio plus venture repo.

G5  Plan objects: a PRD becomes a ticket set

The design shows: one conversation producing N draft tickets in dependency order, struck or kept per line, filed atomically as a set. Exists: depends_on in the ticket schema and parser; release-plan.ts (a different plan: releasing a held lane). Build: a composer plan draft type, a batch filing action (one branch, N ticket files, one PR), and per-ticket source citations back into the PRD's sections. Suggest: FB-134.

G6  The pixel-agents feed

The design shows: the office, live, on every screen: agents at desks while working, reading while researching, a raised hand when waiting on the founder. Exists: run reports per wake and heartbeats; nothing live, nothing visual. Build: a read-only state feed from the venture box (lane state to office scene mapping) and the pixel-agents renderer embed; the studio consumes, never writes. Suggest: FB-135. Venture box plus studio embed.

G7  A live desk

The design shows: a desk a founder keeps open all day; the blocker count, office and run log move on their own. Exists: WhileWorking re-reads once a minute, only while something is in flight and visible; the request budget that makes this affordable is FB-083's. Build: extend the same discipline to the desk's panels, or replace polling with the G6 event stream once it exists. Suggest: fold into FB-135; no separate ticket if the stream lands first.

G8  The pocket studio

The design shows: the one-column mobile companion; a push the moment the founder becomes the blocker. Exists: the FB-009 mobile pass (usable at 390×844); no notifications of any kind. Build: a PWA shell and one push event, founder-became-blocker, from the attention queue. Nothing else pushes; the queue is the only thing that waits on a person. Suggest: FB-136.

G9  The memory library write path

The design shows: add a document from the desk; it lands in what the venture knows, with "last used" citations per document. Exists: the composer reads a document per message (FB-078) and forgets it; D8 defines context/ and library/ in the venture repo. Build: a persistent upload path into the venture repo (heavy binaries to object storage per D8), gbrain indexing on write, and usage citations surfaced back. Suggest: FB-137.

G10  Per-surface outcomes

The design shows: the company by surface: the app running, the last send's numbers, paid growth once connected. Exists: nothing; there is no analytics source. Build: decide the source (venture app events, or the G2/G3 connectors' own reporting) before any ticket; the desk renders whatever reports exist and shows nothing invented. Suggest: a decision memo; FB-138 after.

3 — Suggested order

1. G1 and G4 first: they are studio-side, built on events that already exist, and they are what makes the desk honest.
2. G5 next: the PRD flow is the founder's highest-leverage act and needs only the studio and git.
3. G6 with G7: the venture box work; the office is the product's feeling and the desk's reason to stay open.
4. G2 per the ratified research; G8 and G9 alongside, they are small.
5. G3 and G10 last: both wait on decisions, and the design already tells the truth while they do.
