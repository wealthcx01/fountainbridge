# Marketing pages

Adapted from `engineering/marketing-pages` in [`dzhng/skills`](https://github.com/dzhng/skills) (MIT).

> A **rulebook, not a workflow**: every rule is simultaneously a constraint to build against and a check to
> audit against. **Classify the page first** — every public page belongs to exactly one page class, and the
> class decides its indexing, linking, chrome and wiring.

## Why this is here before it is needed

**It is dormant on purpose.** The Foundry has no public pages yet: Phase 4a is content and site, Phase 5 is
the public front door in Holy Corner. Nothing in the studio today is indexed, and everything is behind
sign-in.

It is taken now rather than later for one reason: **the first marketing page is the one most likely to be
written in a hurry**, by somebody who wants it live today. A rulebook that already exists gets read; one
that has to be found does not. Its description is narrow, so it stays out of the way until a public page is
actually being written.

## The part that will matter first

**Classify before writing.** A campaign lander, a product page, a programmatic tree and an editorial piece
want different indexing, different navigation and different measurement. Writing one and deciding later
what it is produces a page that is all four badly.

## Where our own rules override it

- **Never reproduce another product's copy**, even as a placeholder. The standing rule written for
  cofounder.co applies to every source, and it is copyright rather than taste.
- **Written for the founder** (non-negotiable 12). A marketing page is the one surface where the temptation
  to write cleverly is strongest and the cost is highest.
- **Anything that sends, posts or collects an address is an external action** and gates on a recorded
  approval (non-negotiable 4). A form on a public page is not a page feature; it is a send waiting to
  happen.
- **Interest-based only.** The ratified go-to-market research settles this: we contact people who have
  flagged interest, never cold.
